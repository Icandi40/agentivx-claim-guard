## What is measured

Each record is one agent reply plus what the agent had: its sources, any live status
facts, and whether it had tools. Every sentence is labelled `supported` or `unsupported`.
The guard runs once per reply. A removed sentence is a positive prediction.

* **Precision**: of the sentences the guard removed, the share that were unsupported.
* **Recall**: of the unsupported sentences, the share the guard removed.
* **False-removal rate**: of the supported sentences, the share the guard removed.

All replies and sources are synthetic: fictional companies, `example.com` domains and
555 phone numbers. Nothing comes from real users, clients or private notes. The labels
are one annotator's judgement per split, and the replies were written to resemble model
output rather than sampled from a live agent, so treat the numbers as a description of
this set, not a forecast for your traffic.

## What a pattern-based guard cannot catch

The guard reads surface patterns: action verbs and tense, specific values, status words
next to a named system, and word overlap with sources. That design needs no model or
network calls, gives the same answer every time, and is easy to audit. It also sets hard
limits, and the misses listed above show each of them:

* **Wrong general statements.** "The warranty covers water damage" has no number and
  no action verb. If a source says the opposite, the guard cannot tell. The
  `general_claim` row above shows how many of these it missed.
* **Right value, wrong role.** If the source says Plan A is $20 and Plan B is $35, the
  sentence "Plan A costs $35" passes, because $35 is in the sources. The guard checks
  that a value exists, not what it is attached to.
* **Actions outside the verb list.** First person claims are matched against a fixed
  list of action verbs, so "I reconnected it" and "I blocked the sender" pass. So do
  claims with no action verb at all, such as "Seat 12A is yours".
* **Counts, durations and measurements.** The detail check covers prices, percentages,
  dates, links, file names, emails and phone numbers. "Within 3 days", "10 MB" and
  "12 cities" are not checked, and neither are clock times such as "7:40".
* **Agents with tools.** With `tools_available=True` the guard keeps action claims, since
  text alone cannot show whether a tool call happened. Checking those claims needs the
  tool log, compared call by call.
* **Live facts that are not on/off.** It reads power, lock and door states. A status like
  "recording" or "68 degrees" is not compared.
* **Sources that are themselves wrong.** A recent source with a wrong price makes the
  wrong price look supported.

For those, add a model-based check (an LLM judge or an entailment model) after this
guard, and keep the guard as the cheap first pass whose removals are easy to explain.
